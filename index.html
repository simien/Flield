How to use Flield

Step 1 of 13

What's a flow field?

In generative art, a flow field is a grid of directional forces that steers noise into organic drifts instead of static. Every layer here follows its own field to shape density into bands and clusters, which is where the name comes from.

Random

Step 2 of 13

Random rerolls the canvas

One press, or the R key, and every unlocked setting gets a fresh value. It's the fastest way to see what the tool does; the rest of this tour is about steering it.

General Layer A Layer B

Step 3 of 13

Three tabs, one canvas

General holds the canvas settings and Motion; Layer A and Layer B hold each layer's own look.

Flow scale

Step 4 of 13

Each layer has its own settings

Layer A and Layer B each get their own seed, color, density, flow field, and symmetry, so they rarely match.

Web Tablet Tile

Step 5 of 13

Pick a size, or tile it

Web, Tablet, and Mobile presets set common dimensions; a phone held upright starts on Mobile so the artwork fills the screen. Tile squares the canvas and mirrors both layers so it repeats with no visible seam.

Step 6 of 13

Mask it into a shape, or a fade

An optional shape mask clips or fades the composition, circle, diamond, vignette, corner or side vignette, or stripes, all dithered rather than sharply cut off at the edge.

Lock from randomization

Step 7 of 13

Lock a field to keep it

A locked field holds its value through a randomize pass instead of getting a new one. Lock All / Unlock All apply to everything at once, and since neither can be walked back they arm on a one-second hold rather than a press. A tab shows a padlock once any of its fields are locked.

Density Range

Step 8 of 13

Narrow the range, keep the theme

Palette Range and Density Range constrain how far a randomized color or fill can wander, keeping Auto-randomize inside one tight, cohesive family.

Step 9 of 13

Explore with Auto-randomize

Lock what you like, then slowly unlock the rest while it plays (Space starts and stops it), watching results narrow toward a collection worth keeping. Prefer more control? Skip the button and reach for Random by hand instead.

Step 10 of 13

Undo steps back

Went too far? Undo (or Cmd/Ctrl+Z) walks back through recent passes, whether they came from a press or from autoplay; a GIF export counts as one step, not one per frame. There is no way forward again, so the button arms on a one-second hold. The shortcut is immediate.

Saved

Step 11 of 13

Save it, or share a link

Three save slots live in your browser. Loading one, or saving over one that already holds something, arms on a one-second hold, since both replace work you cannot get back; the first save into an empty slot is a plain press. Copy Link encodes the same snapshot, locks and motion included, into a URL anyone can open, and the menu beside it can make that link offer Auto-randomize.

Step 12 of 13

Export PNG, SVG, or GIF

PNG and SVG save the still. A GIF records however many passes you ask for: with Motion off, that is the sequence Auto-randomize would generate, one still per pass, each held for the speed beside the play button. A whole export is capped at 240 frames, so a long one trades smoothness rather than size.

Drift

Step 13 of 13

Animate it

Motion, in the General tab, moves the composition instead of replacing it. Loop, Drift, Wind, and Pulse each hold every setting and run one cycle that lands back on its first frame, with no cut. Pick one and the canvas plays it straight away; a cycle lasts as long as the speed beside the play button. Leave Auto-randomize running as well and each pass rerolls before it animates. The GIF button records whatever you are watching.

Export & share

Flield is a free, browser-based generative pixel art tool. It creates three-color compositions from seeded flow fields and symmetry, with locks to hold the fields you like while the rest randomize, and export to PNG, SVG, or an animated GIF. Runs entirely in the browser, no account or upload needed.
